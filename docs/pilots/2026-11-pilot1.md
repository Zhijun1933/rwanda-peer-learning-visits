# Pilot round 1: machine-coded sample (5 November 2026)

**Status: exploratory. Machine-coded, not yet checked by a human coder.**
Counts here are for designing the next round, not for citing as findings.
Coded quotations are kept privately and are not in this repository.

## Question

How does Rwanda use study visits to project an image of **sovereign policy
autonomy** and to establish its **status as a development leader** among
African states? (V. Waweru, 5 Nov 2026.) Codes are grouped into dimensions
in [`../analytic_codebook.md`](../analytic_codebook.md).

## Sample

26 sources (24 readable), drawn from `data/visits.csv` / `data/sources.csv`:

- All sources for four visits covered by both Rwandan and non-Rwandan
  outlets: V005 (Somalia), V012 (Lesotho), V019 (Nigeria), V056 (Togo).
- Then one source per visit, at random within strata (seed 20261105):
  East Africa civilian 4, West 3, Southern 3, Central 2; defence colleges
  East 1, West 2, Southern 1.
- Languages: English 18, Kinyarwanda 7, French 1.
- Sources: S017, S018, S019, S030, S031, S036, S037, S038, S053, S057, S063, S071, S075, S081, S092, S097, S102, S123, S127, S128, S130, S138, S140, S145, S147, S153.
- Unreadable: S036, S037 (body not retrievable). S018 and S057 had no
  codable passage (a photo story and a one-sentence brief).

## Method and its limits

Each source was read through a web-fetch tool and coded by a language model
against the codebook. The tool returned paraphrases plus exact excerpts of
up to ~125 characters, so passages are excerpt-level, not full sentences,
and speaker tags were assigned from the tool's rendering. Two spot checks of
excerpts against the live pages matched. No inter-coder reliability yet.
Nigeria is over-represented (5 of 26), as in the full dataset.

## What the pilot suggests (to test, not to cite)

1. **Status, not autonomy, is what these reports carry.** Status codes: 67
   applications; autonomy codes: 1. Home-grown authorship (A3) and rebuttal
   of external criticism (D4) never appeared. Either autonomy is claimed in
   other genres (speeches to donors, policy documents) or visit reporting
   does not carry it. The next round should add official speeches and
   Rwanda Cooperation material to tell these apart.
2. **Status is mostly spoken by guests.** Of status-coded passages, 39 come
   from guests and 6 from Rwandan hosts; narrators (often the host
   institution itself) add 20. Hosts more often describe the visit as an
   "exchange of experiences", which the current codebook cannot code.
   Status may be conferred by proxy: the host stages, the guest says it.
3. **Defence-college tours are staged differently.** They are dominated by
   apparatus (itinerary, sites) and legitimation codes, with little status
   language (6 of 33 applications, against 61 of 103 for civilian visits).
4. **Who pays disappears in Rwandan coverage.** For the Togo visit (V056),
   the AfDB release names the Bank as facilitator and funder; the Kigali Today
   report of the same visit does not mention it and frames the visit as
   Togolese admiration. Autonomy-limiting evidence (C5) appeared only in
   non-media or international sources.
5. **B1 (emulation intent) and B4 (status deference) travel together**
   (Jaccard 0.53). In the next round they may be better read as one
   cluster.
